## 4. Voice & quality

- Audience: parents and caregivers, often newly overwhelmed. Warm, calm, practical, plain language (8th–9th grade reading level). Short paragraphs (1–4 sentences). Use "your child".
- Respectful, neurodiversity-aware language. Autism is not a disease to be cured. Say "support", "build skills", "reduce barriers", "meaningful goals". Never: "cure", "recover", "fix", "guaranteed", "100%", "normal child". Mention assent/child dignity where relevant.
- Evidence-aligned hedging: "research supports", "many children", "results vary", "your child's BCBA will...". No promised outcomes.
- Useful specifics: examples of goals, what a day looks like, questions to ask, checklists, tables where helpful.
- Avoid AI filler: no "In today's fast-paced world", "navigating the journey", "it's important to note", "delve", "game-changer", "unlock", "embark". No rhetorical-question openers. No emoji.
- Local: mention Westminster / Orange County only where it helps the reader (resources, next steps). Do not stuff.
- Length follows intent: 1,500–2,500 words typically; don't pad.

## 5. Structure of each article

- Do NOT write an H1 in the body (the layout renders `h1`). Start with a 2–3 sentence intro that answers the query directly (featured-snippet friendly), then a short "Key takeaways" bullet list (3–5 bullets) as a `> **Key takeaways**` blockquote.
- H2/H3 headings in sentence case, descriptive, naturally containing semantic variations.
- 4–8 internal links in the body using the URLs above (descriptive anchor text, never "click here"). Always link the most relevant service page at least once and /contact/ once near the end.
- Cite sources inline as markdown links on the claim, AND list them in the `references` frontmatter (the layout renders a References section — do not write a References heading in the body).
- FAQs go ONLY in `faqs` frontmatter (3–5 genuinely useful Q&As, answers 2–4 sentences, not duplicating body headings verbatim). Do not write an FAQ heading in the body.
- End the body with a short H2 like "Next steps" / "Talk with our team" with a soft CTA: free consultation, phone (714) 709-7819, link to /contact/. No pressure language.
- Where the topic is medical, add one sentence reminding readers to talk with their pediatrician or a qualified professional about their child's specific needs.
