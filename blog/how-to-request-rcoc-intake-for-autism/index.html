How to Request Regional Center Intake for Autism in Orange County

By United Autism Therapy Clinical Team Published November 9, 2026 6 min read

Quick answer

To request intake with the Regional Center of Orange County (RCOC), choose the age page that fits your child: Early Start for birth to 36 months, or Lanterman for age 3 and older. Then send an inquiry through RCOC's website or call the Intake and Assessment Unit at (714) 796-5354. RCOC's pages do not state intake timelines, so ask the intake specialist.

Asking for help from a regional center can feel like one more form in a long pile of paperwork. The Regional Center of Orange County (RCOC) is the agency that coordinates developmental services for people in Orange County, and it is separate from therapy providers such as United Autism Therapy. You can contact RCOC yourself, and you do not need a provider to do it. This guide explains how to request intake, what to choose first, and what to ask on the phone.

Key takeaways

  • RCOC uses different intake pages by age: Early Start (birth to 36 months), Lanterman (age 3 and older), and Provisional Lanterman (ages 3 and 4).
  • You can send an inquiry through RCOC’s website or call the Intake and Assessment Unit at (714) 796-5354.
  • A physician can send RCOC’s referral form, and the family then contacts the intake unit.
  • RCOC’s pages do not give intake timelines, so ask the intake specialist what happens next.
  • Your child must live in Orange County for RCOC to serve them.

What RCOC is and why families contact it

RCOC is one of the regional centers in California. Its intake team decides whether a person qualifies for regional center services and, if so, helps the family plan what comes next. Regional center services are different from a therapy program: the regional center determines eligibility and coordinates services, while therapy is delivered by separate providers. Think of intake as the door that can open access to services, not the therapy itself.

Autism is one of the developmental disabilities that RCOC serves, and RCOC’s Lanterman pages name it directly. Eligibility is still decided against the criteria on the age page that applies to your child, so the first step is to figure out which page that is. If you are working through an autism evaluation at the same time, the guide to when to get an autism evaluation can help you decide which questions to bring to your child’s doctor.

Step 1: Choose the right age page

RCOC’s intake page directs families to one of three pages, based on your child’s age:

Age RCOC page What it covers
Birth to 36 months Early Start Infants and toddlers who may qualify because of a developmental delay or a known risk condition
Age 3 and older Lanterman Eligibility under the Lanterman criteria, which name autism and set functional limits
Ages 3 and 4 Provisional Lanterman An RCOC option listed for children in this age range

Each page has its own eligibility rules. The early intervention guide explains the birth-to-3 system in more depth, and Lanterman eligibility for autism covers the criteria for children age 3 and older. Start with the page that matches your child’s current age, and ask the intake specialist if your child is between categories.

Step 2: Send an inquiry or call the intake unit

RCOC lists two ways to start:

  1. Use the inquiry form on the RCOC website. The Early Start and Lanterman pages both direct families to an inquiry form to apply or request information from an Intake Specialist.
  2. Call the Intake and Assessment Unit at (714) 796-5354. RCOC’s intake page lists this number for an initial assessment. The general RCOC line is 714-796-5100.

Either route works. A phone call lets you ask questions right away, while the form creates a written record of your request. If you use the form, keep a copy or a screenshot of what you sent.

Step 3: Consider a referral from your child’s doctor

A physician or other healthcare provider can complete RCOC’s Referral for Services Form. RCOC’s intake page says that after the referral, the patient or parent contacts the Intake and Assessment Unit. A referral is helpful if your child’s doctor has already raised developmental concerns, but it is not required for you to make your own inquiry.

If your child’s pediatrician has recommended an evaluation, ask whether they can complete the referral form at the same time. Bringing the doctor’s notes or developmental history to the intake call can help the intake specialist understand your child’s needs.

Step 4: Prepare for the first call

Before you call, gather a few basics so the conversation is productive:

  • Your child’s full name, date of birth, and home address in Orange County
  • The name of your child’s doctor and any diagnosis or referral letter you have
  • A short list of your top concerns, such as communication, play, self-care, or behavior
  • Any evaluation reports, school plans, or therapy notes you already have
  • Questions you want answered, written down

RCOC’s pages do not list required documents, so the intake specialist will tell you what they need. Ask for the list in writing, and ask whether you can send copies by email or through a secure upload.

Step 5: Ask the right questions

Many parents feel unsure what to ask an intake specialist. These questions keep the call focused:

  • Which age page should my child use, and why?
  • What happens next after I send the inquiry or referral?
  • Who will contact me, and when?
  • What documents do you need from me?
  • Can I get the information packet in my preferred language?
  • Who is my contact person if I have questions later?

RCOC’s Lanterman page notes that its information packet, which describes the regional center system and individuals’ rights, is available in several languages. Ask for the version in your language before you start, so you can read it at your own pace.

Common mistakes to avoid

Waiting for a perfect diagnosis before calling. You can ask for intake while an evaluation is still in progress. The intake team can explain how their process fits with your child’s medical and school evaluations.

Assuming the regional center is the same as a therapy provider. RCOC and a provider such as United Autism Therapy play different roles. Keep notes from each conversation so you know who is responsible for each step.

Not writing down names and dates. Record the name of each person you speak with, the date, and what they said. If you are asked to send a document, write down the date you sent it.

Assuming there is no time limit. RCOC’s pages do not give timelines, which means you should ask for them directly. Do not wait for a call back without following up.

What to do while you wait

Many families keep therapy and school steps moving while the regional center process is underway. Speaking with a qualified professional now can help your child start building skills and can give you concrete information for the intake team. Common options include:

  • A pediatric check-up to review developmental concerns and any screening results
  • An IEP request if your child is school-age and you suspect they need support at school
  • A review of your insurance and coverage options, as explained in how to start ABA through insurance
  • Exploring in-home ABA therapy or ABA therapy to see whether a program fits your child’s needs

Waiting for intake does not have to mean waiting for help. Many families combine these steps.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a doctor's referral to contact RCOC?

No. RCOC's intake pages say families can request information or apply through the inquiry form on the RCOC website, or call the Intake and Assessment Unit. A physician can also complete RCOC's referral form for services, and the family then contacts the intake unit.

What is the difference between Early Start and Lanterman at RCOC?

RCOC lists Early Start for infants and toddlers from birth to 36 months, and Lanterman for people age 3 and older. RCOC also lists Provisional Lanterman for children who are 3 or 4 years old. Each page has its own eligibility criteria, so choose the page that matches your child's age.

Does my child have to live in Orange County?

Yes, RCOC serves people who live in Orange County. If your child lives in another county, RCOC's pages direct you to the state directory to find the regional center that serves your area.

How long does regional center intake take?

RCOC's intake pages do not state timelines for intake, assessment or eligibility decisions. Ask the intake specialist at your first call what the next steps and expected timing are, and write down the name of the person you spoke with.
