Autism in Orange County: Answers to the Questions Parents Ask Most

By United Autism Therapy Clinical Team Published November 3, 2026 5 min read

Quick answer

If you're concerned about autism in Orange County, start with your pediatrician, who can screen your child and refer you for a full evaluation. Children under 3 can be evaluated through the Regional Center of Orange County's Early Start program without a diagnosis. From age 3, school districts can assess for special education. Health plans, including Medi-Cal through CalOptima Health, can cover therapies like ABA when medically necessary.

If you’re wondering about autism in Orange County, you’re not alone, and help is closer than it may feel. This page answers the questions Orange County parents ask most, in plain language: how common autism is, what to watch for, who diagnoses it, and where to find services and support locally. Each answer starts with the short version, followed by details and links.

Key takeaways

  • Start with your pediatrician. They can screen your child and refer you for an evaluation.
  • Children under 3 can be evaluated through RCOC’s Early Start program, without a diagnosis.
  • From age 3, your school district can assess your child for special education.
  • Medi-Cal (CalOptima Health) and many private plans can cover therapy such as ABA when medically necessary.
  • Free parent support is available through RCOC’s family resource center and local advocacy groups.

How common is autism?

About 1 in 31 eight-year-old children in the U.S. has been identified with autism, according to the CDC’s monitoring data from 2022. Autism occurs in all racial, ethnic, and income groups.

In practical terms, many Orange County classrooms, neighborhoods, and parent groups include autistic children. If you’ve noticed differences in your child’s development, asking questions early is a common and sensible step.

What are the early signs of autism?

Common early signs include limited eye contact, not responding to their name by about 9 months, few gestures like pointing or waving by about 12 months, and strong reactions to sounds, textures, or changes in routine. The CDC lists signs and symptoms by age.

Every child develops differently, and no single sign means a child is autistic. What matters is a pattern over time. Our article on early signs of autism goes into detail.

When should my child be screened?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends autism-specific screening at the 18-month and 24-month well-child visits. Its 2020 clinical report also recommends developmental surveillance at every routine visit.

You don’t have to wait for a scheduled visit. If you have concerns at any age, ask your pediatrician for a screening.

Who diagnoses autism in Orange County?

Autism is usually diagnosed by a specialist, such as a developmental-behavioral pediatrician, child psychologist, child psychiatrist, or pediatric neurologist, often after a referral from your pediatrician. The CDC describes how diagnosis works: there is no blood test, so specialists look at your child’s development and behavior.

In Orange County, families usually take one of these paths:

  • Through your health plan: your pediatrician refers you to an in-network specialist.
  • Through RCOC: for children under 3 via Early Start, or for older children who may qualify for Lanterman services.
  • Through your school district: from age 3, the district can assess for special education eligibility. This is an educational determination, not a medical diagnosis.

Wait times vary, so it’s worth starting more than one path at once. See when and how to get an autism evaluation.

What does the Regional Center of Orange County do?

The Regional Center of Orange County (RCOC) coordinates services and supports for people with developmental disabilities and their families. It is a nonprofit contracted by the State of California, based at 1525 N Tustin Ave in Santa Ana. Its main number is 714-796-5100.

  • Early Start (birth to 36 months): evaluation and early intervention for babies and toddlers with developmental delays or risk factors. A diagnosis is not required.
  • Lanterman services (age 3 and older): for people with qualifying developmental disabilities, including autism.

Our Orange County autism resource guide walks through RCOC eligibility step by step.

What therapy options are available?

The most common autism therapies are ABA (applied behavior analysis), speech-language therapy, and occupational therapy, delivered at home, in a center, or at school. Many children use more than one.

Our guide to autism therapy in Orange County compares each option, the different settings, and the questions to ask providers.

Does insurance cover autism therapy in Orange County?

Often, yes. In Orange County, Medi-Cal is provided through CalOptima Health. Medi-Cal covers behavioral health treatment, such as ABA, for members under 21 when medically necessary. Many private plans regulated in California also cover autism treatment.

Coverage details, prior authorization, and in-network providers vary by plan. See does Medi-Cal cover ABA therapy? and how to get ABA covered by insurance.

What help do Orange County schools provide?

From age 3, public school districts can assess autistic children for special education and create an IEP (Individualized Education Program) with services such as speech therapy, occupational therapy, and behavior support. These services are free to families.

To start, send your district a written request for a special education assessment. Our IEP guide for Orange County parents covers the steps and California timelines.

Where can Orange County parents find support?

Several local organizations offer free help to families:

  • RCOC’s Comfort Connection Family Resource Center connects families of children with special needs to resources and support, free of charge.
  • TASK (Team of Advocates for Special Kids) offers parent support, IEP consultations, and workshops in English and Spanish.
  • Other parents. Many families say talking with another parent who’s been through it is the most helpful first step.

I’m worried about my child. What should I do this week?

  1. Write down what you notice, with examples and dates. Short videos can help too.
  2. Call your pediatrician and ask for a developmental or autism screening.
  3. If your child is under 3, call RCOC about an Early Start evaluation. You don’t need a diagnosis.
  4. If your child is 3 or older, consider a written request to your school district for a special education assessment.
  5. Call your health plan to ask about evaluation and therapy coverage.

You don’t need to have every answer before you take the first step.

Frequently asked questions

How common is autism?

The CDC estimates that about 1 in 31 eight-year-old children in the U.S. has been identified with autism, based on 2022 data from its Autism and Developmental Disabilities Monitoring Network.

At what age should my child be screened for autism?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends autism-specific screening at the 18-month and 24-month well-child visits, along with general developmental monitoring at every visit. You can ask for a screening any time you have concerns.

Who can diagnose autism in Orange County?

A diagnosis usually comes from a specialist such as a developmental-behavioral pediatrician, child psychologist, child psychiatrist, or pediatric neurologist. Your pediatrician can refer you, and your health plan can tell you which specialists are in network.

Does my child need a diagnosis to get help in Orange County?

Not always. Children under 3 can be evaluated through RCOC's Early Start program based on developmental delays or risk, without an autism diagnosis. School districts can also assess children from age 3 for special education. Health plans often require a diagnosis or referral for ABA.

What is the Regional Center of Orange County?

RCOC is a nonprofit contracted by the State of California to coordinate services and supports for people with developmental disabilities. It runs Early Start for children under 3 and provides Lanterman services for eligible people age 3 and older. Its main number is 714-796-5100.

Is autism therapy covered by insurance in Orange County?

Often, yes. Medi-Cal, provided in Orange County through CalOptima Health, covers behavioral health treatment such as ABA for members under 21 when medically necessary. Many private plans regulated in California also cover it. Check your plan for details.
