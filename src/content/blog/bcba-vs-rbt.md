The main difference between a BCBA and an RBT is their role on your child's ABA team. A BCBA (Board Certified Behavior Analyst) assesses your child, designs the treatment plan, and supervises the team. An RBT (Registered Behavior Technician) works with your child directly during sessions, following that plan under close supervision.

> **Key takeaways**
>
> - Both credentials come from the Behavior Analyst Certification Board (BACB).
> - A BCBA holds a graduate-level certification; an RBT holds a paraprofessional certification.
> - The BCBA plans, supervises, and reviews data. The RBT delivers sessions and records data.
> - RBTs always work under close, ongoing supervision.
> - You can check any BCBA's or RBT's certification on the BACB Certificant Registry.

## What is a BCBA?

A BCBA is a Board Certified Behavior Analyst. The BACB describes the BCBA as a [graduate-level certification in behavior analysis](https://www.bacb.com/bcba/). It also states that BCBAs are independent practitioners who can provide behavior-analytic services and supervise the work of RBTs and other professionals who carry out behavior-analytic interventions.

To earn the credential, a candidate must meet the eligibility requirements in the BACB's BCBA Handbook, apply, and pass a certification exam. BCBAs must then maintain and renew their certification.

### What does a BCBA do in ABA therapy?

On a child's team, the BCBA usually:

- **Assesses your child** to learn their strengths, needs, and interests
- **Writes the treatment plan**, including specific goals and teaching strategies
- **Trains and supervises** the RBTs who work with your child
- **Reviews data** to see whether goals are moving and adjusts the plan
- **Meets with you** to talk about progress and coach you on strategies at home

The BACB's [Ethics Code for Behavior Analysts](https://www.bacb.com/ethics-information/ethics-codes/) asks behavior analysts to involve families in selecting goals, designing interventions, and monitoring progress. It also asks them to use data to decide whether to continue or change services. We explain that process in [ABA data collection](/blog/data-driven-aba-therapy/).

## What is an RBT?

An RBT is a Registered Behavior Technician. The BACB describes the RBT as a [paraprofessional certification in behavior analysis](https://www.bacb.com/rbt/). A **paraprofessional** is a trained practitioner who works under the direction of a more advanced professional.

According to the BACB, RBTs [assist in delivering behavior-analytic services and practice under the direction and close supervision](https://www.bacb.com/rbt/) of an RBT Supervisor and/or RBT Requirements Coordinator who is responsible for their work. To earn the credential, a candidate must meet the eligibility requirements in the RBT Handbook, apply, and pass an exam. RBTs must then meet ongoing supervision and renewal requirements to keep it.

### What does an RBT do in ABA therapy?

The RBT is often the person your child sees most. During sessions, an RBT usually:

- **Works one-on-one with your child** on the goals in the treatment plan
- **Uses the teaching strategies** the BCBA has chosen, such as prompting and positive reinforcement
- **Records data** on each goal during the session
- **Shares what they notice** with the BCBA, such as a new skill or a hard moment

The BACB's RBT Ethics Code says RBTs [follow the direction of their supervisors](https://www.bacb.com/ethics-information/ethics-codes/), accurately carry out behavior-technician services, and accurately complete required documentation. It also says RBTs bring questions or concerns from others, including caregivers, to their supervisor.

## BCBA vs RBT: side-by-side comparison

| | BCBA | RBT |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Board Certified Behavior Analyst | Registered Behavior Technician |
| Credential level (per BACB) | Graduate-level certification | Paraprofessional certification |
| Practices independently? | Yes | No, always under close, ongoing supervision |
| Assesses your child | Yes | Supports the BCBA as directed |
| Writes and changes the treatment plan | Yes | No, follows the plan |
| Works directly with your child | Sometimes, during observations and training | Yes, in most sessions |
| Collects session data | Sometimes | Yes |
| Reviews data and makes decisions | Yes | Reports observations to the BCBA |
| Trains and coaches parents | Usually | May model strategies during sessions, as directed |
| Supervises others | Yes, including RBTs | No |

## How BCBA supervision of RBTs works

Supervision is what keeps your child's plan on track between team meetings. The RBT Ethics Code says RBTs only provide services [within a clearly defined role under close, ongoing supervision](https://www.bacb.com/ethics-information/ethics-codes/). The Ethics Code for Behavior Analysts includes a full section on supervision, covering topics like performance monitoring and feedback.

In practice, supervision often includes:

- The BCBA watching sessions and giving the RBT feedback
- Reviewing data together and updating teaching steps
- Training the RBT on new goals or strategies
- Checking that your child is comfortable and engaged

The BACB sets specific supervision requirements for RBTs, which are detailed in its RBT Handbook. Exact schedules vary by program and by your child's needs, so it is a fair question to ask any provider.

## How a BCBA and an RBT work together in in-home ABA

With [in-home ABA therapy](/services/in-home-aba/), you often get to see this teamwork up close. Here is how a typical arrangement might look:

1. **The BCBA visits** to assess your child and learn about your family's routines.
2. **The BCBA writes the plan**, with goals such as asking for help or getting through bath time with less stress.
3. **The RBT runs regular sessions** in your home, teaching those goals during play and daily routines.
4. **The BCBA returns** to observe, coach the RBT, review data, and talk with you.
5. **The plan changes** as your child learns and as your family's priorities shift.

The CDC notes that in ABA, [progress is tracked and measured](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/treatment/index.html). That steady loop of teaching, measuring, and reviewing is what the BCBA-RBT partnership is designed to support. For a look at what happens during a typical visit, see [what happens in an ABA therapy session](/blog/what-happens-in-an-aba-therapy-session/).

## Why this team structure matters for your child

The two-level model gives your child both expertise and consistency.

- **Expert planning.** A BCBA with graduate-level training in behavior analysis decides what to teach and how. When something is not working, they have the background to figure out why.
- **Consistent, frequent practice.** The RBT is often the person your child sees most during the week. That steady relationship can help your child feel comfortable and gives skills many chances to grow.
- **A built-in check.** Because the RBT follows a written plan and records data, and the BCBA reviews that work, decisions are less likely to rest on one person's impression.

For parents, it also means you have two kinds of support. The RBT can show you what a strategy looks like in a real moment, such as a bedtime routine. The BCBA can explain the reasons behind it, help you set priorities, and offer [parent training](/services/parent-training/) so you can use the same approach between sessions.

A good team also talks openly with you. If the RBT and the BCBA ever seem to give you different messages, ask both of them to meet with you so everyone is on the same page.

## How to check a BCBA's or RBT's credentials

You can search the [BACB Certificant Registry](https://services.bacb.com/o.php?page=101135) by certification number, or filter by credential type, status, and location. It covers BCBAs, RBTs, and other BACB credentials.

When you talk to any provider, these questions can help:

- Is my child's program designed and supervised by a BCBA?
- Are the therapists who work with my child certified RBTs?
- How often will the BCBA observe sessions and meet with me?
- How will the BCBA and RBT share updates with me?
- Who do I contact with questions between sessions?

For a full checklist, see [how to choose an ABA provider](/blog/how-to-choose-an-aba-provider/). If you are new to ABA, start with [what ABA therapy is](/blog/what-is-aba-therapy/).

Every child is different, so talk with your pediatrician or a qualified professional about your child's specific needs.
