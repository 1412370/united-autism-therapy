Early Start ends on your child's third birthday. After that, a child in Orange County may move to school special education, to regional center services, to both, to provisional regional center eligibility, or to neither. Each has its own plan and its own paperwork, and the planning starts well before the birthday.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Early Start serves children from birth to 36 months, so services end at age 3.
> - Planning should start at least 6 months before the birthday. A transition meeting should happen at least 90 days before it.
> - Schools write an IEP. The regional center writes an IPP. A child can have one, both, or neither.
> - Provisional eligibility is a middle option for ages 3 and 4 at the regional center.
> - RCOC offers a "Moving On At 3" workshop for parents. Its main line is 714-796-5100.

## What actually changes at 36 months

The [Regional Center of Orange County (RCOC)](https://www.rcocdd.com/client-services/intake/early-start/) runs Early Start for children from birth to 36 months. The state's Early Start Information Packet calls the end of those services the [transition at age 3](https://www.rcocdd.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/SIP_Early-Start_English.pdf). This guide uses the April 2023 edition of that packet. Rules and wording can change, so check with your service coordinator for anything that's time-sensitive.

Early Start isn't something that just rolls over. Your child's services come from a different system after 3, with different eligibility rules and a different plan. And two agencies may be involved, the school district and the regional center.

If you've already read about [early intervention for autism](/blog/early-intervention-for-autism/), think of this article as the next chapter. That post explains why starting early matters. This one explains what happens on the other side of the third birthday.

## The possible outcomes at age 3

The state packet lists the results families can end up with. Here they are in plain words.

| Outcome | What it means | Plan |
|---|---|---|
| School special education only | The school district finds your child eligible. The regional center doesn't. | IEP |
| Regional center only | The regional center finds your child eligible. The school doesn't. | IPP |
| Both | Your child is eligible with both agencies. | IEP and IPP |
| Provisional eligibility | For ages 3 and 4 with significant limitations in two life areas. Ask RCOC which areas apply. | Ask RCOC |
| Neither | Your child doesn't qualify for either one. | None |

Two notes on that table. First, the packet says that for eligible children, most services come through special education from age 3, even when a child isn't in a preschool classroom. So the school side is often the bigger piece. Second, eligibility with one agency doesn't decide the other. They use different tests.

## IEP and IPP: two plans with different jobs

An IEP, or individualized education program, is the written plan your school district creates for special education. An IPP, or individual program plan, is the plan your regional center creates. They sound alike, but they cover different parts of life.

The state packet describes it this way. School services are for educational benefit. The regional center supports life at home and in the community. That's also a simple way to think about who provides what. The school side focuses on what your child needs to learn at school, and the regional center side on supports for home and community life. Health insurance is a separate track again, so ask each agency what it will and won't provide.

For the school side in more depth, including timelines and what goes in the document, see our guide to the [IEP process for autism in Orange County](/blog/iep-for-autism-orange-county/). We won't repeat it here.

### How regional center eligibility works after 3

After age 3, RCOC uses what it calls Lanterman eligibility. RCOC lists [autism as a qualifying condition](https://www.rcocdd.com/client-services/intake/lanterman/). The disability has to begin before age 18, be expected to last indefinitely, and be substantial. RCOC defines substantial as significant limitations in at least three of seven areas: self-care, language, learning, mobility, self-direction, capacity for independent living, and economic self-sufficiency. The child must also live in Orange County.

So a diagnosis alone doesn't settle it. What counts is how the condition affects daily functioning. Our post on [regional center eligibility for autism](/blog/regional-center-eligibility-autism/) walks through that test in more detail.

### What provisional eligibility means

RCOC's intake page names [provisional Lanterman eligibility for ages 3 and 4](https://www.rcocdd.com/client-services/intake/). The packet describes it as an option for children with significant limitations in two named life areas. It can last until age 5. At that point the child must meet the full Lanterman criteria, or the services end. Ask your regional center which two areas apply to your child.

## The transition planning meeting

Per the 2023 packet, planning should start at least six months before your child's third birthday. At least 90 days before the birthday, you and your service coordinator meet someone from your child's school for a transition planning conference.

Think of it as a planning step. The packet says the plan covers how to move toward special education, regional center eligibility, and other services such as Head Start, State Preschool, First 5, Help Me Grow and health insurance.

A few things can make the meeting more useful:

- Write down your questions ahead of time, and bring them on paper.
- Ask your service coordinator who from the school will attend.
- Ask what evaluations the school and regional center will each do, and when.
- Ask what happens to current services while decisions are pending.
- Ask for the next steps and who to call, in writing if you can.

If you feel your child's birthday is close and nobody has mentioned a meeting, call your service coordinator and ask. Don't wait for a letter.

For families whose child isn't yet connected to RCOC, our guide on [how to request RCOC intake for autism](/blog/how-to-request-rcoc-intake-for-autism/) covers the first call.

## The Moving On At 3 workshop

RCOC offers a "Moving On At 3" workshop for parents. According to the [2026 flyer](https://www.rcocdd.com/wp-content/uploads/FRC/2026/MovingOnAt3AllYear2026EN.pdf), it covers assessment and eligibility for school district services, the first IEP meeting, and ongoing regional center eligibility. The flyer also says translation may be provided if you ask in advance.

Dates change, so check the RCOC site for current dates. If you can attend before your transition meeting, it's a good way to arrive with questions already in hand.

## What if you disagree, or your child doesn't qualify

Say a school team tells you your child doesn't qualify, or the regional center offers provisional eligibility when you hoped for full. It isn't always the end of the road.

Here's what you can do:

1. Ask for the reasons in plain language, and ask what they looked at.
2. Ask whether your child could qualify with the other agency. The two decisions are separate.
3. Ask about the community options in the transition plan, like Head Start, State Preschool, First 5 and Help Me Grow.
4. Talk with your pediatrician about what you're seeing at home and what other supports might help.
5. Reach out to local parent groups. Our list of [autism support for Orange County parents](/blog/autism-support-orange-county-parents/) is a place to start.

We haven't covered formal dispute steps here, because the sources we checked don't spell them out. Ask the school and RCOC how to disagree with a decision, and ask for that information in writing.

## Where ABA may fit once your child is 3 or older

Some families also look at applied behavior analysis (ABA), a therapy that builds skills through structured teaching and reinforcement, once their child is 3 or older. It's one possible support next to school and regional center services, and it isn't required. If you're curious, our overview of [ABA therapy](/services/aba-therapy/) explains how it works, and your child's team can help you decide what fits.

## Who to call in Orange County

Start with these:

- **Your Early Start service coordinator.** The 2023 packet says you and your service coordinator meet someone from the school for the transition planning conference.
- **RCOC main line: 714-796-5100.** This is the number on RCOC's Early Start and Lanterman pages.
- **Your local public school.** The CDC says [children 3 and older are evaluated through the local public school](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/early-intervention/index.html).
- **Your pediatrician.** They know your child's history and can talk through next steps.

Keep a simple folder. Put in every letter, evaluation report, and the names and dates of your calls. It sounds small, but it saves you time at every meeting.
