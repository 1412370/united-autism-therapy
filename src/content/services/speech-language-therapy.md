## Helping your child communicate in their own way

Communication is more than talking. It is how your child tells you they are hungry, shares something exciting, asks for help or says “no.” Speech and language therapy helps children develop verbal and non-verbal communication so they can express themselves and be understood.

According to the [American Speech-Language-Hearing Association (ASHA)](https://www.asha.org/public/speech/disorders/autism/), speech-language pathologists work with autistic children to assess communication and social skills, teach new skills, and recommend and teach augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) when it helps.

## Areas speech therapy can support

- **Understanding language (receptive language)** — following directions, understanding questions and concepts
- **Using language (expressive language)** — words, phrases and sentences to share wants, needs and ideas
- **Social communication** — taking turns in conversation, joint attention, play and reading social cues
- **Speech sounds** — making sounds and words clearer
- **Non-verbal communication** — gestures, signs, pictures and body language
- **AAC** — picture systems, communication boards or speech-generating devices

## Speaking, non-speaking and everything in between

Every child communicates differently. Some children speak in full sentences but find conversation hard. Others use a few words, repeat phrases they have heard, or do not use spoken words yet. Speech therapy meets your child where they are.

If your child is not speaking yet, AAC can give them a way to communicate now. Many parents worry that AAC will stop their child from learning to talk. ASHA’s guidance is that AAC does not prevent speech development. Our article on [AAC and autism](/blog/aac-and-autism/) explains how it works and how families can get started.

## Working together with ABA

Many of our families also receive [ABA therapy](/services/aba-therapy/). When speech and ABA teams share goals, your child gets consistent practice across sessions — for example, using the same words or AAC symbols to request a favorite toy during both. We coordinate so communication goals are reinforced rather than competing.

## Practicing at home

Some of the most powerful communication practice happens during everyday moments. You will get simple ideas to use during meals, bath time, play and car rides. For a head start, read our guide to [speech therapy for autism](/blog/speech-therapy-for-autism/), which includes activities you can try at home.
