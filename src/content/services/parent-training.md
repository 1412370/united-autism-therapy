## You are the most important person on your child’s team

Therapy sessions are a small part of your child’s week. The rest happens at home, in the car, at the park and at grandma’s house. Parent training gives you practical tools to support your child in all of those moments — and helps you feel more confident while you do it.

At United Autism Therapy, parent training is part of every ABA program. It is led by your child’s Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) and built around your family’s real life, not a generic curriculum.

## What parent training covers

Topics are chosen with you, based on your child’s goals and what feels hardest right now. Families often work on:

- **Understanding behavior** — what a behavior might be communicating, and what tends to happen before and after it
- **Encouraging communication** — creating reasons for your child to communicate, and responding in ways that build on it
- **Routines** — visual schedules, “first–then” language and predictable steps for daily tasks
- **Transitions** — preparing your child for changes, leaving the park, turning off a screen
- **Reinforcement** — noticing and celebrating what is going well
- **Challenging moments** — staying calm, keeping everyone safe and responding consistently
- **Community outings** — errands, appointments and family events

## How sessions work

We follow a simple cycle: explain, show, practice and give feedback. Your BCBA explains a strategy in plain language, shows it with your child, then coaches you as you try it. You will not be handed a stack of worksheets and left to figure it out.

Training can happen during your child’s sessions, at our Westminster center, at home or as separate caregiver meetings. Other important adults in your child’s life, like a grandparent or nanny, can often join so everyone is on the same page.

## Support for you, too

Caring for a child with autism can be joyful and demanding at the same time. Parent training is also a place to ask questions, share what is working and talk about what feels hard. For more ideas, read [how parents can support ABA therapy at home](/blog/supporting-aba-therapy-at-home/) and our local guide to [autism support for parents in Orange County](/blog/autism-support-orange-county-parents/).
